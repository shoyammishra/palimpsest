\begin{abstract}
Data order changes optimization trajectories, but endpoint divergence alone does not reveal how the corresponding trace develops or whether it is recoverable. We measure output divergence and raw parameter-space endpoint-direction alignment along public Pythia-160M/PolyPythias trajectories trained on 300 billion tokens. Three correlated order-only pairs reach symmetric KL of 0.290--0.293 nats, approximately 3.8 times a late-training adjacent-checkpoint drift reference, compared with 0.306 nats for one joint initialization-and-order pair. The central finding is a temporal dissociation: at step 16,000, the order/joint divergence ratio is already 0.932 for one pair, while its raw endpoint-direction cosine is only 0.294. These are distinct readouts, not a causal decomposition. Most parameter-block grafts then leave a predeclared language-modeling loss band, and none of the tested Fisher estimators predicts all three probe directions within 25\% relative error. The comparisons use one training-distribution shard and lack an initialization-only control. Persistent order dependence is measurable; functional recoverability and the cost of rewriting completed optimization histories remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Training is an ordered composition of update maps: identical initial states and data multisets can yield different completed histories. Endpoint distance leaves several questions separate. Does an output-level difference persist? When does its magnitude become substantial, and when does the raw parameter-space endpoint-difference direction align with its final direction? Can that trace be corrected, and can a proposed recovery probe be trusted?

A scalar output divergence need not track parameter orientation: later dynamics may change a displacement's direction while preserving the scale of its effect on predictions. We examine this distinction using public Pythia and PolyPythias trajectories \citep{pythia,polypythias}, without new pretraining. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Temporal dissociation.} Functional divergence is near its late-training scale while the raw endpoint-difference direction remains weakly aligned: at step 16,000, the order/joint ratio is 0.932 and the cosine is 0.294 for the primary pair. Qualitative agreement across correlated pairs has pair-dependent timing.
\item \textbf{Persistent output differences.} Three correlated order-only comparisons reach 0.290--0.293 nats at the final Pythia-160M checkpoint. These are pairwise comparisons sharing one initialization, not independent replicates.
\item \textbf{Validity-gated recovery analysis.} The tested graft class and Fisher estimators fail declared validity criteria. Their failure prevents recovery claims, rather than establishing irreversibility.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dynamical framework and measurement design}
\paragraph{Transported order defects.}
Let $x$ include parameters and any optimizer state, and let $U_i$ be one update map. The adjacent-swap defect is
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{ij}(x)=U_j(U_i(x))-U_i(U_j(x)).
\end{equation}
For smooth SGD updates $U_i(x)=x-\eta g_i(x)$,
$\delta_{ij}(x)=\eta^2(Dg_j(x)g_i(x)-Dg_i(x)g_j(x))+O(\eta^3)$.
Thus the leading local defect is the established commutator term \citep{rukhovich,sweeney}. Stateful optimizers require the augmented state; the parameter-only SGD expansion cannot be applied unchanged to Adam.

Connect two update words by adjacent swaps. For swap $k$, let $z_k$ be the state after its first ordering, $z_k+\delta_k$ the state after its alternative, and $A_k$ their common remaining update composition. Their endpoint increment is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_k=A_k(z_k+\delta_k)-A_k(z_k),\qquad
 x_T^{(B)}-x_T^{(A)}=\sum_k\Delta_k.
\end{equation}
The second identity telescopes over intermediate words with a common initial state and the same update maps. Differentiability gives $\Delta_k\approx DA_k(z_k)\delta_k$: later dynamics can amplify, attenuate, or rotate local defects, which can also cancel in the sum. Checkpoint differences below are direct observations, not reconstructed per-swap attributions. The identity does not identify when Pythia's individual order effects were generated.

\paragraph{A restricted correction floor.}
For a parameter gap $\Delta$, fixed linear correction subspace $S$, and fixed quadratic metric $\Sigma\succeq0$, define
\begin{equation}
 r_S(\Delta)=\min_{c\in S}\|\Sigma^{1/2}(\Delta-c)\|_2.
 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
Every correction $c\in S$ has residual norm at least $r_S(\Delta)$, directly by minimization; the minimum is attained since $\Sigma^{1/2}S$ is a finite-dimensional subspace. This elementary conditional floor is neither a nonlinear irreversibility theorem nor a computational lower bound, and does not cover state-dependent reachable spaces. Section~4 tests the metric prerequisite; no functional correction floor is measured here.

\paragraph{Models, metric, and controls.}
Write $m,d_1,d_2$ for the main Pythia-160M run and its two data-seed variants. They share an initialization. We analyze $P_1=(d_1,d_2)$, $P_2=(m,d_1)$, and $P_3=(m,d_2)$; these are three correlated pairs from three runs, not three independent replicates. The joint arm $J$ uses the two full-seed variants, changing initialization and order together. The endpoint is $T=143{,}000$ updates (300B training tokens).

For next-token distributions on a frozen shard $E$, our direct readout is
\begin{equation}
 D_E(a,b)=\frac{1}{2|E|}\sum_{x\in E}
 [\KL(p_a(\cdot|x)\|p_b(\cdot|x))+\KL(p_b(\cdot|x)\|p_a(\cdot|x))].
\end{equation}
The shard contains 48 non-overlapping 2,048-token blocks from \texttt{NeelNanda/pile-10k}, concatenated with EOS separators using the Pythia tokenizer: 98,304 prediction positions. It samples the training distribution, not a held-out generalization benchmark. Models run in evaluation mode with float32 forward passes and float64 accumulation. We compare 12 checkpoints from step 0 to $T$, excluding step 1 after a checkpoint-integrity audit. The candidate initialization-only arm failed continuity checks and was excluded before the amended comparison; $D_E(J)-D_E(P_i)$ confounds initialization and initialization--order interaction. The amended order-only criterion is weaker than the original two-arm criterion.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Local Lie-bracket criteria predict order effects in domain and sequential learning \citep{rukhovich,sweeney}; mode-connectivity work studies instability to training noise \citep{frankle}. Pythia/PolyPythias enable trajectory and seed comparisons \citep{pythia,polypythias}. Here the focus is direct output-distribution divergence between completed histories, its timing relative to raw endpoint-direction alignment, and probe validity before recovery claims. We claim neither novelty for order sensitivity itself nor a new general transport theorem.

\paragraph{Late-training order effects persist.}
The final order-only divergences are 0.290--0.293 nats (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}), compared with 0.306112 for $J$. All exceed the late adjacent-checkpoint drift reference, 0.076275, and lie below the 160M--410M comparison, 0.460360. Their maximum/minimum ratio is 1.009, below the predeclared threshold of 2.0. \paragraph{The central dissociation: magnitude precedes raw alignment.}
For $P_1$, let $\Delta_t=\theta_{d_1,t}-\theta_{d_2,t}$ and $C_t=\langle\Delta_t,\Delta_T\rangle/(\|\Delta_t\|_2\|\Delta_T\|_2)$. At 16,000, $D_E(P_1)=0.312597$ nats, already near its final scale, while $C_t=0.294$. This statement holds in absolute KL, without the joint denominator. The ratio $R_t=D_E(P_1,t)/D_E(J,t)$ is 0.932. At 64,000, $R_t=0.943$ and $C_t=0.877$; the first sampled $C_t\geq0.9$ occurs at 128,000. The ratio is not monotone: it falls from 0.993 at 128,000 to 0.949 at $T$.

At 16,000, $P_2$ and $P_3$ have raw cosines 0.441 and 0.466, and both first cross 0.9 at 64,000. The same qualitative dissociation occurs in all three correlated pairs, with pair-dependent timing. \textbf{Functional divergence magnitude and raw parameter-space endpoint orientation evolve on different timescales.} This is a descriptive comparison, not a test of functional-direction lock-in. Early directions are storage-quantization limited; $C_T=1$ is an identity and nearby checkpoints correlate mechanically. Threshold-crossing times are grid-dependent, not precise stabilization times.

The drift reference is not a noise floor. It depends on training time and checkpoint interval: over 1,000-update windows it is 0.680225 at 1,000--2,000 and 0.140831 at 16,000--17,000. Early pair values do not establish separation from drift; the 3.8-fold comparison applies only late in training. Neither a scalar ratio nor raw cosine identifies an output-space direction or recoverability.

\section{Recovery claims require validated probes}
\paragraph{Parameter-type grafts leave the allowed loss region.}
Raw alignment does not measure recovery. At $T$, we form hybrids $\theta_h=\theta_{d_1}+\alpha M_b(\theta_{d_2}-\theta_{d_1})$, where $M_b$ selects a parameter-type block and $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,1\}$. The frozen rule rejects token cross-entropy above the worse parent's loss plus 0.5 nats: a threshold of 3.001560. Instrument checks passed, including parent-divergence reproduction to relative error $1.4\times10^{-10}$.

Seven of eight complete block swaps are invalid. The recorded loss-band tally rejects 23 of 35 evaluated graft readouts, including control and second-anchor readouts of the same hybrids. Attention QKV, carrying 87\% of the squared parameter gap, is invalid at every tested nonzero strength. Only internal LayerNorm remains valid through a full swap; that hybrid is degraded and far from both parents. The frozen abort rule stops the remaining control sequence, leaving spatial recoverability inconclusive. \emph{Parameter-space graftability cannot be assumed to measure functional recoverability}: this graft class fails at the tested strengths, without ruling out other edits.

\paragraph{Local Fisher metrics do not certify.}
To attempt Eq.~\eqref{eq:floor} without grafts, we test $D_E(\theta,\theta+v)\approx v^\top\Sigma_Ev$, with $\Sigma_E=\tfrac12\widehat F$. The predictive Fisher gives the local quadratic approximation under our half-sum KL convention, not generally the observed-label loss Hessian. Empirical and predictive Fisher are distinct \citep{kunstner}; substitution needs validation.

The frozen gate requires relative prediction error at most 0.25 on all three scaled probe directions: random, the last two checkpoints' update, and the endpoint order gap. Cross-entropy increases are 0.0050, 0.0337, and 0.0087 nats, respectively. Table~\ref{tab:fisher} reports the relative errors. No family passes; the planned projection ladder and Fisher-weighted direction timing are therefore unrun.

The predictive diagonal has 13\% error along the endpoint gap, yet underpredicts update-direction KL by about 5.3 times. \emph{Agreement along the selected order-gap direction does not certify a local metric.} The pattern is consistent with missing cross-parameter curvature; it does not establish a unique cause. Independent builds reproduce the order-gap pass/random fail pattern, and the direct divergence readout reproduces to relative error $1.42\times10^{-10}$. Small loss changes alone do not certify the quadratic regime; finite perturbations and estimator sampling remain limitations. No tolerance was relaxed.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
Order-only output differences persist, with substantial magnitude before raw parameter-space endpoint-direction alignment. The failed validity checks separate observing a trace from measuring its recoverability. Scalar functional divergence, raw parameter direction, functional geometry, and actual correction are distinct objects; none substitutes automatically for another.

Evidence is limited to one model size, three correlated order-only pairs sharing one initialization, one joint pair, and one training-distribution shard. Initialization-only effects and interactions cannot be isolated; independent-run statistical variation and downstream generalization are unmeasured. No universal irreversibility, successful functional transport, computational lower bound, or validity of arbitrary Fisher models follows. A next experiment should certify a metric with cross-parameter structure before projection/recovery claims, with probe criteria frozen anew. Low-rank proposals must test directions outside their represented span. Functional recoverability and cheap history correction remain open.
